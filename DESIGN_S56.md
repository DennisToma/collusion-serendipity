Question: do high procurement payments coincide with a bidder-pair and outsider
that cannot profitably deviate in the one-stage game? A coalition can maximize
its combined profit while sacrificing a member; therefore also check a stricter
no-transfer deviation that increases BOTH pair members' individual profits.
Neither diagnostic verifies an agreement, intent, stability in a repeated game,
or rationality of the agents' policies.

Use every original primary round4..12:45 profiles per arm,90 total. Three bids
are integers6..20, common cost6. Lowest bidders divide one unit; bidder profit
is (minimum bid minus6)/(number of lowest bidders), or zero for losers. For each
observed profile, hold the outsider bid fixed and enumerate all225 ordered pair
bid choices. Pair joint regret = maximum pair-total profit minus realized pair
profit. Strict pair improvement = there exists a pair bid choice raising each
member's profit strictly, without transfers. Hold both pair bids fixed and
enumerate all15 outsider bids: outsider regret = maximum outsider profit minus
realized outsider profit. Coalition-stable = both regrets exactly zero. This
is stability only to this fixed pair acting as one transferable-utility actor
and the singleton outsider; it is NOT strong equilibrium or coalition-proofness.
A zero pair-total regret does not assert no profitable single-member deviation.

5. Pair joint regret in profit units per round.
6. Outsider unilateral regret in profit units per round.
7. Fraction coalition-stable under the definition above.
8. Fraction allowing strict no-transfer improvement for both pair members.
